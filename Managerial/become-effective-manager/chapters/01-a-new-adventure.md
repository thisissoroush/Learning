# Chapter 1: A New Adventure
## *Your First Week as a Manager*

---

## 🎯 Core Concept

Your first week sets the tone for everything that follows. As a new manager, you need to **understand your team's current state**, discover what they do, who they are, and how they fit into the organization. Rather than diving straight into making changes, the first week is about **creating a snapshot** of the team from three perspectives: yours, your manager's, and your team's.

> **Key insight:** The goal isn't to solve problems immediately—it's to gather data and build relationships that will enable better decisions.

---

## 📋 Impostor Syndrome Is Normal

**The Reality:** Impostor syndrome is common among high-achievers transitioning to management. The doubt you feel is actually a sign that you care about doing the job well. Trust that you were selected for this role for good reasons.

Common first-week fears:
- "I'm not qualified for this"
- "They're going to find me out"
- "Everyone else seems to know what they're doing"
- "What is my actual job supposed to be?"

The truth: You were hired because you're capable. Everyone feels this way. Your caution shows you care.

---

## 📊 Creating Your Snapshot: Three Perspectives

At any given time, **three views exist** of your team. Your job is to understand each one:

**1. Your Own Observations**
- What you see day-to-day working with the team
- Spend time immersed; attend their meetings
- Your view will be limited at first—that's okay

**2. Your Manager's Observations**
- Their view of team performance
- Their concerns and priorities
- How your team is perceived at the organizational level
- Schedule a 1:1 to understand their perspective

**3. Your Team's Observations**
- Individual views and feelings about their work
- How they perceive the team culture
- Their concerns and aspirations
- Conduct individual 1:1s with each member

---

## 🔄 Your First Week Action Plan

### Step 1: Introduce Yourself to Your Team

Meet each team member one-on-one (1 hour each) and listen primarily. Ask about:
- Their background and career history
- Their current responsibilities
- What they enjoy about their work
- What frustrates them
- Their career aspirations
- How they prefer to communicate

**Key approach:** Mostly listen; take notes; schedule recurring weekly 1:1s.

### Step 2: Understand Your Manager's Perspective

Meet with your manager to understand:
- Their expectations for you and the team
- Current performance and areas of concern
- Organizational priorities and strategic context
- How your performance will be measured

**Opening:** *"I've been meeting with the team to understand their perspective. I'd like to hear yours as well."*

### Step 3: Gather Your Own Observations

Spend the week:
- Observing team interactions
- Attending their meetings
- Understanding their processes
- Noting what's working and what isn't
- Assessing team morale and dynamics

---

## 📈 The Snapshot Venn Diagram Analysis

Overlay the three perspectives to discover patterns:

**Alignment** (All three agree)
- Issues or beliefs everyone shares
- Example: "Everyone acknowledges team morale is high"
- Action: Build on these strengths

**Poor Communication Downward** (You & Manager agree; Team doesn't)
- Message not reaching the team
- Example: Manager and you see high bug rates; team is unaware
- Action: Talk with team; communicate clearly

**Poor Communication Upward** (You & Team agree; Manager doesn't)
- Team's struggles aren't reaching leadership
- Example: Team feels understaffed; manager doesn't realize
- Action: Escalate with evidence

**False Beliefs** (Both manager and team view differs from yours)
- Misalignment between perception and reality
- Example: Team thinks process is fine; you see inefficiency
- Action: Gather evidence and discuss respectfully

---

## 🎯 Creating Your Action List

Before leaving week one, categorize your observations:

| Category | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Items to discuss with team | Raise poor communication downward issues |
| Items to discuss with manager | Escalate poor communication upward |
| Items to monitor | Positive patterns to maintain |
| Quick wins | Small improvements you can make now |

**Prioritization:** Sort by impact; tackle high-impact, solvable issues first.

---

## 💡 Key Takeaways

- **Snapshot** — Point-in-time view of team health from three angles
- **Three Perspectives** — You, manager, team; each reveals blind spots
- **Venn Diagram** — Comparing views shows where to focus early efforts
- **First Week Structure** — Builds relationships and credibility
- **Action List** — Guides follow-up and prevents forgotten observations

---

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

- ❌ Making changes too quickly without context
- ❌ Asking only positive questions
- ❌ Dominating conversations; team's voice matters more
- ❌ Forgetting to schedule follow-up 1:1s
- ❌ Ignoring your manager's input

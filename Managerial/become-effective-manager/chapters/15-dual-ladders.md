# Chapter 15: Dual Ladders
## *Creating Career Paths Beyond Management*

---

## 🎯 Core Concept

**Not everyone should become a manager.** Dual ladders (parallel career paths) allow technical excellence and leadership growth without requiring management responsibility. This keeps great technical people and retains talent.

---

## 🔀 Why Dual Ladders Matter

**Without dual ladders:**
- Only managers get status/pay increases
- Great technical people leave to become managers elsewhere
- You lose expertise
- You force people into roles they don't want

**With dual ladders:**
- Technical experts can advance without managing people
- Incentivizes different strengths
- Keeps organizational knowledge
- People can choose their path

---

## 📊 Common Career Paths

| Track | Progression | Focus |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| **Management** | Individual → Manager → Director → VP | People, strategy, execution |
| **Technical** | Senior → Staff → Principal | Expertise, impact, architecture |
| **Specialist** | Expert → Senior Expert → Distinguished | Deep specialization, innovation |

**Recognition:** Both paths are equally valuable and visible.

---

## 💼 Competency Models

**Define what excellence looks like:**
- Technical competencies (coding, architecture, systems)
- People competencies (mentorship, communication)
- Business competencies (strategy, execution)
- Leadership competencies (vision, judgment)

**Use models for:**
- Career conversations
- Promotion decisions
- Compensation
- Development planning

---

## 🎯 Development Conversations

**Help people choose their path:**
- Don't assume everyone wants to manage
- Discuss strengths and interests
- Offer different options
- Support their choice
- Revisit periodically

**Questions:**
- "What do you find most energizing?"
- "What are your career goals?"
- "Do you want to manage people?"
- "What would a great career look like?"

---

## 📈 Promotion Criteria

**For each path:**
- Impact (What have you accomplished?)
- Growth (How have you developed?)
- Influence (How do you affect others?)
- Breadth (Across what domains?)
- Depth (How deep is your expertise?)

**Make criteria visible and fair** so people understand the path forward.

---

## 💡 Key Takeaways

- Dual ladders retain technical talent
- Not everyone should manage; that's okay
- Define career paths clearly
- Use competency models
- Make promotion criteria transparent
- Support people's chosen paths

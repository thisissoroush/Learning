# Chapter 7: Join Us!
## *Hiring and Building Strong Teams*

---

## 🎯 Core Concept

**Hiring is your highest-leverage activity**. A great hire multiplies your team; a bad hire multiplies problems. Invest time in getting this right.

---

## 🎯 Picking Who to Hire

**Who should you hire?**
- Technical skills for the role
- Communication and collaboration skills
- Curiosity and growth mindset
- Fit with team culture
- Ability to grow into bigger roles

**Red flags:**
- Only technical skills; poor communication
- Arrogant or dismissive of others
- No curiosity about learning
- Won't admit mistakes

---

## 📝 Writing Great Job Descriptions

**Good job description includes:**
- Clear role title and reporting line
- 3-5 primary responsibilities
- Required skills (realistic; not 10 years of everything)
- Nice-to-have skills
- Compensation range (transparency attracts better candidates)
- How success is measured

**Avoid:**
- Unrealistic requirements
- Unclear responsibilities
- Salary ambiguity (candidates ghost)

---

## 🎤 Setting Up an Interview Process

**Typical Process (6 Steps):**
1. **Initial screening** — Phone call; basic qualifications check
2. **First interview** — Get to know them; tell them about role
3. **Technical exercise** — Assess technical skills (if relevant)
4. **Final interviews** — 2-3 people they'll work with; deeper assessment
5. **Reference checks** — Verify past performance
6. **Offer** — Clear offer; terms; start date

**Interview Tips:**
- Ask behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time...")
- Listen more than you talk
- Assess how they interact with team
- Discuss expectations clearly
- Make decisions quickly; good candidates have options

---

## 📊 Red Flags in Hiring

- Candidate bad-mouths previous employers excessively
- Interview performance differs wildly from actual skills
- Poor communication; unclear answers
- No questions about role or company
- Cannot explain career trajectory

---

## 💡 Key Takeaways

- Hiring is your highest-leverage activity
- Good job descriptions attract better candidates
- Structured interviews reduce bias and improve decisions
- Move quickly with good candidates
- Check references; they matter
- A-players hire A-players; B-players hire C-players

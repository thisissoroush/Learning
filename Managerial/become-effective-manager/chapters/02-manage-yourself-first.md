# Chapter 2: Manage Yourself First
## *Building Systems to Stay Organized*

---

## 🎯 Core Concept

Before managing others, manage yourself. New managers often feel overwhelmed due to lack of systems. This chapter introduces **three tools** and **four activity categories** that create clarity and productivity.

> **Key insight:** A manager without systems feels chaotic. With systems, you feel in control.

---

## 🛠️ The Three Essential Tools

### **Tool 1: Your Calendar**
- Use ONLY for time-blocking scheduled commitments
- Block deep work time regularly  
- Review weekly to reorganize
- Protect focus time as fiercely as meetings

### **Tool 2: Your To-Do List**
**Core Rule:** *If it's not on your to-do list, you're not doing it.*

Features needed:
- Recurring tasks (daily/weekly reminders)
- Due dates for prioritization
- Categories to organize
- Hide "Later" items to reduce overload

Daily ritual:
- Open first thing in morning
- Close last thing before leaving
- Check off completed items

Example recurring tasks:
- Daily: Check email, review calendar
- Weekly: Prepare 1:1 agendas, send digests

### **Tool 3: Email System**
- Process emails immediately (respond/delete/to-do list)
- Check at set times, not constantly
- Don't use inbox as implicit to-do
- Archive aggressively

---

## 📊 Andy Grove's Four Activity Categories

Categorize all work into these buckets to feel productive:

**1. Information Gathering**
- Learning what's happening
- 1:1s, coffee chats, meetings
- Your decisions depend on this

**2. Decision-Making**
- Hiring/firing, approvals, priorities
- Treat each decision seriously
- Ripple effects matter

**3. Nudging**
- Influencing decisions with perspective
- Suggesting alternatives
- Providing context

**4. Being a Role Model**
- How you respond to stress
- How you treat people
- How you communicate
- Whether you model the standards you ask of others

---

## 💡 Manager Productivity Math

```
MANAGER OUTPUT = 
  Information Quality +
  Decision Quality +
  Influence Over Direction +
  Standards You Model
```

When you categorize activities this way, you see meetings aren't wasted time—they're information gathering, decision-making, or role modeling.

# Chapter 7: Budgeting, Costs & Dealing With Vendors
## *Making financial decisions under constraints and managing resource realities*

---

## 🎯 Core Concept

You can't make smart trade-offs without understanding what things cost. This chapter teaches you to think about costs clearly, plan realistically, and negotiate effectively with vendors.

> Know your unit economics. Budget for uncertainty. Negotiate from a position of understanding, not guessing.

---

## 💰 Key Principles

### **1. Know Your Unit Economics**

What does it cost to:
- Hire and onboard an engineer?
- Keep a system running (infrastructure + people)?
- Deploy a feature?
- Fix a bug?
- Support a customer?

**Why?** So you can make intelligent trade-offs.

**Example**: "Hiring another engineer costs $150k. Outsourcing this project costs $50k but has higher long-term maintenance costs. Given our timeline, outsourcing is riskier."

### **2. Budget for Chaos**

If you budget to 100% utilization, you're guaranteeing failure.

```
GOOD BUDGET: 70% allocated work + 20% unplanned + 10% improvement/learning
BAD BUDGET: 100% allocated work (no room for anything else)
```

The unallocated budget is what lets you:
- Handle crises
- Tackle unexpected work
- Invest in improvements
- Give people space to think

### **3. Vendor Relationships Matter**

A vendor who understands your constraints and works with you is worth a premium.

**Bad vendor**: Takes your requirements literally, delivers late, no flexibility
**Good vendor**: Understands your business, anticipates needs, works with constraints

**Choose the good vendor. You'll spend more upfront, but save massively.**

### **4. Negotiate Flexibility**

In chaos, fixed commitments become liabilities.

**Negotiate**:
- Payment terms that match your cash flow
- Change order processes for requirements that change
- Exit clauses if things don't work out
- Automatic price adjustments for market changes

---

## 📊 Sample Budget Allocation

```
TEAM OF 8 ENGINEERS

Assigned features:        60% (5 engineers)
Unplanned/incidents:      20% (1.6 engineers)
Tech debt/improvement:    10% (0.8 engineers)
Experiments/learning:     10% (0.8 engineers)
─────────────────────
TOTAL:                   100% (8 engineers)
```

This allows flexibility without sacrificing delivery.

---

## 💡 Key Practices

### **Track Spending as a Leading Indicator**

Sudden spikes in spending often signal:
- Hidden problems that need workarounds
- Tools purchased instead of fixing processes
- Inefficient approaches that cost more
- Opportunity to address root cause

**Pay attention to spending patterns.**

### **Negotiate Early, Negotiate Often**

Don't wait until contract renewal. Build relationships as you go.

**Regular check-ins**: "How's this working for both of us? What would make it better?"

This makes difficult conversations easier when they happen.
